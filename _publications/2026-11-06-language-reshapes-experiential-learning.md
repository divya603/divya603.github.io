This preprint introduces Instructed Bandit Tasks, a set of eight sequential decision-making problems in which participants earn rewards while receiving natural-language hints about the task environment. Across two behavioral experiments (N=337 and N=300), hints improved performance and changed how participants made choices.

The effects are modeled with a hierarchical Bayesian reinforcement learning agent that treats language as a probabilistic constraint over beliefs, and compared with Centaur, a language model fine-tuned on human behavior. Both models predicted which hints would help. Centaur also captured cases where language hurt performance or lost its benefit as people gained experience. The results suggest that normative and data-driven cognitive models can work together to capture the nuances of human learning.
